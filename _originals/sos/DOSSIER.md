## 2. Mission, vision, principles

**Mission** (verbatim, paraphrased): "Work for the right of children, girls and boys to live in a family. Implement family strengthening programs to prevent loss of parental care, and when care has been lost, offer alternative care in family-like environments."

**Three pillars** (from aios_py_qhacemos.html):

1. **Prevención** — strengthen families to prevent separation
   - Daily pedagogical childcare
   - Individual family accompaniment
   - Capacity-building
   - Local network coordination

2. **Cuidados Alternativos** — when prevention fails:
   - Family-style residential care (modelo familia SOS)
   - Foster families
   - Kinship care
   - Adoption advocacy where applicable

3. **Abogacía / Advocacy** — policy work to push the Paraguayan state to strengthen child protection systems

**Guiding principles**: "Last resort" principle for separation (UN Guidelines on Alternative Care), family reintegration priority, "best interest of the child" as compass.

**2030 Strategy** — Federación Internacional launched a refreshed "Strategy 2030" in June 2025; Paraguay began implementing Proyecto Ojoykére in 2024 as the local instantiation.

## 4. Programs (current)

### 4.1 Acogimiento Familiar Residencial
5 aldeas. Each "family house" has a long-term caregiver ("madre SOS" / referente afectivo). Children attend local schools.

### 4.2 Cuidados Alternativos
Beyond the aldeas:
- **Foster care** (familias acogedoras)
- **Kinship care** (with extended family)
- **Adoption advocacy** for children with no possibility of reintegration

### 4.3 Prevención — Proyecto Ojoykéré (Ojoykére)
**Launched 2024.** Prevention + family-strengthening services. Community-level work in vulnerable neighborhoods. Recent activity: **Feria de Servicios** Aug 14 2026 in Tablada Nueva — 300+ attendees. (Note: home page spells it "Ojoykére" but news page spells "Ojoykéré".)

### 4.4 Advocacy
Incidence at national policy level. Cooperation with Universidad de Comillas (Spain) on Latin America standards research. Mention of contributions to international standards for children deprived of family care.

## 8. The 2025 crisis — full timeline

### Background
Hermann Gmeiner founded SOS Children's Villages in 1949 in Imst, Austria, after WWII. He died in 1986. He's been celebrated as a humanitarian icon for decades. The organization operates in 135+ countries with budgets in the hundreds of millions USD.

### Timeline (verified via aios_intl_urgent.html, midiario.com, themunicheye.com, daraj.media, theinternational.at)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Oct 23, 2025 (daytime, AT)** | Austrian media (APA, others) publish investigation revealing historical allegations of **sexual violence and physical abuse** against children by Hermann Gmeiner in Austria between the 1950s-1980s |
| **Oct 23, 2025 (daytime, PY)** | Paraguay issues its own press release **before** the International federation's official statement. Distances itself: "Aldeas Infantiles SOS en Paraguay... legalmente constituida en el país... organización autónoma." Establishes that Paraguay has a "Comité Especial Nacional de Salvaguardia Infantil, Juvenil y de Adultos" |
| **Oct 23, 2025 (night)** | International Board holds **emergency session**. Suspends **SOS-Kinderdorf Österreich** (Austrian association) as member federation. Suspends representative Andreas Kovar |
| **Oct 24, 2025** | International press release issued. Quoting Dominico (Mimmo) Parisi, Board President: "Los corazones de la Junta Internacional están con todos aquellos que sufrieron por las acciones de un hombre... tolerancia cero..." |
| **Oct 24+ 2025** | 8 victims compensated (confirmed via midiario.com) |
| **Pending** | Future Extraordinary General Assembly to vote on removing Austria's designated seat on the International Board |

### Impact on Paraguay
- **Direct operations**: NONE — Paraguay is autonomous, no investigation
- **Reputation**: Significant collateral damage — the brand is shaken globally
- **Donor sentiment**: Likely affected (international padrinos may pause; corporate allies may want reassurance)
- **Comms response**: Paraguay was actually *quicker* than the Federation to publicly respond — defensive posture with full distancing

### What this means for any partnership proposal
- They are fragile, reputation-wise
- Any partnership / cobranded offer must include clear "safeguarding" language
- They are aware — internal Nacional Safeguarding Committee is real

## 10. Strengths & weaknesses (SWOT-lite)

### Strengths
- 55 years of brand trust in Paraguay
- Federation backing (135+ countries) gives international credibility
- Strong corporate ally portfolio (top PY brands)
- Government recognition — likely registered as ONG with the SET, possibly with SNNA (Secretaría de la Niñez) accreditation
- Active program innovation (Proyecto Ojoykére 2024)
- Multiple revenue streams already

### Weaknesses
- Digital infrastructure is broken in places
- No online donation gateway
- No public transparency / annual report
- No donor-facing CRM
- International scandal in 2025 hit global brand
- Limited geographic spread — 5 aldeas don't cover the country

### Opportunities
- 1M+ niños en riesgo in Paraguay (their own stat) — clear TAM for expansion
- "Became padrino" online model — well-established internationally (Compassion, Plan)
- Tigo Money / Pix / Personal Pay integration for donation
- Google Ad Grants ($10k/mo free)
- Crypto donations (controversial; they probably won't)
- "Sponsor a child" microsite
- Volunteer portal (presencial + digital)
- SaaS partnerships (Mailchimp, Donorbox, Funraise — all have NGO programs)
- TikTok creator partnerships with Paraguayan influencers
- Climate-emergency angle (Natural Hazards — Paraguay is increasingly flood-prone)
- Belt-and-suspenders on safeguarding — public report would actually rebuild trust

### Threats
- Donor flight post-scandal
- Competing ONGs with better digital (Fundación Paraguaya, CIRD, CEPEP)
- Paraguay economic slowdown
- Political pressure to privatize child welfare
- Domestic political attacks if the scandal expands
