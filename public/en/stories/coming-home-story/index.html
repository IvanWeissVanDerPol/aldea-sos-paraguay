A story of coming back

A representative case of an alumnus who returns to the village as a volunteer.

Fictionalised story. This narrative is representative of the virtuous circle the organization tries to build and does not identify a real person. The organization publishes real stories with consent on its official site.

📅 Demo · Category: Community · Reading time: 3 min · Voice: composite profile

This story represents the virtuous circle the organization tries to build: young people who grew up in the programme, left it, and come back as volunteers, staff, or mentors.

The alumni network works as a mutual-support ecosystem. Some alumni participate formally as volunteers; others simply keep ties with village staff who accompanied them when they were children.

That continuity is one of the reasons accompaniment doesn't end at 18 — the formal model extends to age 24, and the relationships tend to last much longer.

What this means in practice

For a young person who left the programme, coming back to the village as a volunteer isn't retroactive charity — it's reciprocity. Those who can do it usually do. Those who can't, say so.

The organization asks nothing of them. There's no expectation of "paying back what you received." The idea is that being accompanied naturally reshapes your willingness to accompany others — and that this willingness is there for whenever life's timing allows it.

What if I've been through something like this and want to be part of it?

If you or someone close to you has been through alternative care in Paraguay and wants to connect with the organization's alumni network, the most direct route is to write to the organization itself via its official site.

This demo has no way to connect anyone with anyone. For real contacts, visit aldeasinfantiles.org.py.
